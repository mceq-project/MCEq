Added ``docs/examples/ERA5_density_spline.ipynb``, which converts an ERA5
netCDF download into MCEq tables, and
``docs/examples/AIRS_density_spline.ipynb``, which does the same for the AIRS
satellite retrieval (AIRS3STD 7.0).  The AIRS notebook subsets server-side over
OPeNDAP, so a week of the southern region costs 20 MB instead of 5.1 GB, and
fills the retrieval's gaps -- levels cut off below the terrain, and columns the
retrieval failed on entirely -- which the table format cannot carry.  Every
column is tagged with whether it was measured, taken from the other overpass,
or filled from a zonal mean, so a quoted number is never mistaken for a
measurement it is not.  Its maps and animations are built one atmosphere per
grid node, so the field shown comes from the same interface the solver
integrates against rather than from the file.  The per-cell footprint counts
are mapped, which shows the coverage: Aqua's 98.2 deg inclination turns the
ground track around at 81.8 deg,
so the polar cap is reached only by the swath edge, and the band containing
IceCube is the thinnest-covered part of the table.
The notebook is written for a reader new to both AIRS and MCEq, and leans on
figures rather than prose: schematics for the processing chain, for the four
gap-filling rules, and for the geometry of an up-going shower, alongside the
measured maps and spectra.  It ends at IceCube, using
:class:`~MCEq.geometry.density_profiles.TabulatedLocationCentered` for the
up-going band from 90 to 115 degrees, with the ice surface and the detector
depth given separately, the atmosphere sampled at the shower impact point --
50 degrees of great circle away at the far end of the band, which is why the
downloaded region reaches to 35 degrees S -- azimuth averaging around the
resulting ring, and an NRLMSIS 2.1 tail above the top pressure level (the
optional ``nrlmsis`` package, named in the prerequisites).  Grids of
atmospheres are solved with :meth:`~MCEq.core.MCEqRun.solve_batch`.  The map
and direction grids are deliberately coarse so that the whole notebook runs in
a few minutes; the cells say what to raise for more detail.
