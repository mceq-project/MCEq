Added ``docs/examples/ERA5_density_spline.ipynb``, which converts an ERA5
netCDF download into MCEq tables -- one longitude/latitude grid per day --
compares the result against NRLMSISE-00, and maps and animates it globally with
cartopy, reading every plotted number back through the MCEq atmosphere
interface.  It starts from ERA5's invariant surface geopotential, turning it
into a table of ground elevations and a map of the Earth's terrain, so that
each column is integrated from where the ground actually is rather than from
sea level; the resulting column-depth map is orography, and its animation is
the day-to-day weather on top of it.  It also shows the shower impact point tracking from the detector at
zenith 0° to the antipode at 180°, which is what a gridded table buys for
neutrinos.

The ground is read at the orography's own resolution and aggregated over each
atmosphere cell, rather than point-sampled at the cell centre, and the column
depth is averaged over the cell in :math:`X` rather than evaluated at the mean
elevation.  This is the resolution that matters for overburden: on a 1 degree
grid the nearest single node differs from the cell mean by 20 m for the median
land cell and 257 m at the 95th percentile, which is a column depth wrong by
27 g/cm^2 at the 95th percentile and up to 167 g/cm^2 in the mountains, against
the 0.3-1.7 g/cm^2 that striding the *atmosphere* from ERA5's native 0.25
degree to 1 degree costs.  It is free, because
:meth:`~MCEq.geometry.density_profiles.EarthsAtmosphere.h2X` gives the
overburden above any height for a column already built, so one atmosphere per
node still covers every elevation in its cell.  The notebook now shows what
point sampling would have cost as its own map, and ``dask`` -- needed by the
chunked ``open_dataset`` call it has always made -- is listed among the
requirements.
