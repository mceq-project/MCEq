MCEq v1.4.2
###########

MCEq v1.4.2 is a **database bugfix release**.

| The **Sibyll** hadronic interaction matrices have been **regenerated** with a fixed version of chromo_.
| In v1.4.0 and v1.4.1 neutron projectiles were simulated as protons in the Sibyll models.
| Nothing has to be changed in your code: the corrected database is downloaded automatically the first
  time you import MCEq after the update.

.. note::

   v1.4.1 was a code-only release and ships the **same database file** as v1.4.0. Every ratio shown on
   this page therefore applies to both versions, even where the figures are labelled ``v1.4.2/v1.4.0``.


What has changed?
=================

For a more detailed list please refer to the latest Changelog_.

1. Sibyll interaction matrices

   | The hadronic interaction yields of **Sibyll-2.1**, **Sibyll-2.3d** and **Sibyll-2.3e** have been
   | regenerated with a fixed version of chromo_ (see `chromo PR #275 <https://github.com/impy-project/chromo/pull/275>`_).
   | Neutron projectiles are now treated as neutrons and no longer as protons.

2. Decay yields, cross sections and low energy extension

   | Unchanged in their physics input. The low energy extension below 80 GeV is DPMJetIII-19.3 as before,
   | and the DPMJetIII-19.3 fluxes are identical to v1.4.0 over the whole energy range.

The neutron projectile bug
==========================

The interaction matrices of the v1.4 database are generated with chromo_, which provides a common
interface to the event generators. In chromo's Sibyll interface the projectile was dispatched on
``kin.p1.is_nucleus``. Because the PDG ID scheme describes a single nucleon as a nucleus with mass
number A = 1, this branch was also taken for protons and neutrons. Both were therefore passed to
Sibyll's *nuclear* projectile entry point ``sibnuc(A=1)``, which only carries the mass number and not
the identity of the nucleon. The Sibyll beam codes that distinguish a proton (13) from a neutron (14),
and with them the ``sibyll()`` code path, were never reached. As a result **every nucleon projectile was
effectively simulated as a proton**.

The consequence is a lost isospin asymmetry between proton- and neutron-induced interactions. A proton
projectile produces more π+ than π- (π+/π- ≈ 1.08), while a neutron projectile has to produce the
mirrored yield (π+/π- ≈ 0.97). With the bug both gave ≈ 1.08. Since the nucleon cascade in the
atmosphere carries roughly equal amounts of protons and neutrons, this positive charge excess was
counted for the whole nucleon flux instead of only half of it, which inflated the inclusive muon charge
ratio from ≈ 1.27 to ≈ 1.47.

The fix routes only *real* nuclei (A > 1) to ``sibnuc``, while nucleons go through ``sibyll()`` with
their correct beam code. Proton-induced yields are unaffected by the fix; only the neutron projectiles
change.


Database Comparison
-------------------

The following figures compare the v1.4.2 database against the database of v1.4.1/v1.4.0. Every model is
compared against *itself*, computed with the H3a primary model of crflux_ for a vertical zenith angle
and ``config.enable_em = False``, everything else at the MCEq defaults.

**Sibyll-2.3d and Sibyll-2.3e**: The muon charge ratio drops by **13 %** and the νμ/ν̄μ ratio by
**10 %** between the low energy extension and 1e6 GeV. This is the restored isospin asymmetry of the
nucleon cascade and the main effect of the fix. Their charge summed fluxes stay within 3 %.

**Sibyll-2.1**: The charge and flavor ratios shift by 6 % in the conventional region, but the model
changes drastically in the **prompt region**: at 1e8 GeV the νe flux rises by a factor **4.3** (νμ by
1.44) and the corresponding flavor ratios drop to **0.14** (νe/ν̄e) and **0.46** (νμ/ν̄μ). Note that
Sibyll-2.1 carries no charm, so its prompt νe flux is 50 times below Sibyll-2.3d and 160 times below
DPMJetIII-19.3 in this region. See :ref:`the comparison with v1.3 <v142_vs_v13>` below.

**Other models**: The charge and flavor ratios of QGSjetII04, QGSjetIII, Epos-LHC, Epos-LHC-R and
DPMJetIII-19.3 are unchanged at the sub-percent level. Their charge summed νμ and νe fluxes shift by up
to **+7 %** above 100 GeV, and the muon flux by up to 2 %. DPMJetIII-19.3 is identical to v1.4.0.

**Low E Extension**: Below 80 GeV (dashed line) all models are extended with DPMJetIII-19.3, which is
not affected by the bug. All ratios approach unity there.

**Ultra High Energies**: Towards 1e9 GeV and above the ratios are dominated by statistical fluctuations
from the Monte Carlo generation of the hadronic interaction matrices at high projectile energy.

Relation to the v1.4 vs. v1.3 comparison
----------------------------------------

The Sibyll panels of the :ref:`v14v13_diff` comparison were produced with the v1.4.0 database and are
affected by this bug. The large excursion of Sibyll-2.1 above is a **correction back to v1.3
behaviour**, not a new deviation: in the prompt region v1.4.0 had lost roughly a factor 4 of the
Sibyll-2.1 electron neutrino flux with respect to v1.3.1, which v1.4.2 restores to within 5 %.

Epos-LHC panels of that comparison are not affected.


What does this mean for my results?
===================================

* **Recomputation is recommended** if your results were obtained with Sibyll-2.1, Sibyll-2.3d or
  Sibyll-2.3e from the v1.4.0/v1.4.1 database. Charge and flavor ratios are affected the most, since
  the bug acts on the charge asymmetry of the mesons and not on their total number.
* **Muon fluxes** (charge summed) change by less than 3 % for every model in the database.
* **Prompt fluxes of Sibyll-2.1** change by a large factor and should be recomputed, keeping in mind
  that this charmless model predicts a negligible prompt flux in the first place.
* **DPMJetIII-19.3** results are unchanged.
